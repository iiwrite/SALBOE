We all like to believe that we are in control of what we consume online. After all, we know that the algorithm works on a feedback loop. It takes our data: our clicks, our watch time, our likes, our behaviors to predict what to suggest next. However, whether we like it or not, we don't shape the algorithm; the algorithm shapes us.

We are humans. It is our innate desire to be wanted, to belong, to be validated. The social media companies have weaponized this nature of ours and quantified it with likes and views. Once something is measurable, the numbers start to feel meaningful. The more views and likes a video gets, the more credible and appealing it becomes and consequently, worth imitating. And that's how we fall into trends without ever questioning ourselves: Why?

One of the most irrational things I have seen in the past few months is the trend of Hailey Bieber's comb technique of applying a face mask. The sheer volume of posts, articles, discussions and even news on the matter feels gratuitous given that there is no clear benefit over existing methods. It was shocking to me how quickly people replicated it and it made me ruminate on how so many individuals can take time out of their day to do these trends for the sake of engagement. It is so because likes translate to validation, belonging and provide that dopamine hit to us.

These patterns are not confined to social media trend, they extend far beyond them. These emulations appear in so many forms in our lives: the clothes we wear, the food we eat, the movies we watch, the music we listen to, the pictures we post, the hobbies we pursue, sometimes even the major life decisions we make, without consciously choosing to do so. Because the "mindless" scrolling we do for hours actually has an impact on us and it is not mindless after all.

When we like a video, the algorithm responds by showing us more of the same. The same aesthetic, lifestyles, opinions, edits are presented before us over and over again and block out the other. Over time, this constant repetition can start to feel familiar and familiarity often feels like preference. The line between what we actually like and what we like because the internet currently likes becomes blurred.

Taking into example: let's say you liked a video of a girl making iced coffee, then another of people romanticizing drinking coffee as the first thing in the morning, another and then another. Gradually, your brain starts to associate coffee with productivity and the aesthetics of being "that girl" and not with its taste. And you fall into this trap of drinking coffee whether you like it or not, just for the purpose of belonging, aesthetics and productivity.

I am not here to criticize you for following trends or make a statement that every influence is inherently bad. We need influence because that's how we learn and explore. It becomes only a problem when we let it control the way we choose to live.

Also, I am not here to give you advice on how to be that person with niche hobbies. Whether you like the most influential pop star who is selling out stadiums or that underrated indie band with only a thousand followers, I will criticize you either way if you are borrowing that preference through imitation rather than reflection of your inner self.

So next time, ask yourself:

*Do you really like the bitter taste of coffee that doesn't sit right with you, no matter how much sugar you add or do you like it because of how coffee is associated with productivity and aesthetics?*

*Do you really enjoy the matcha or do you enjoy the feeling of taking that Pinterest-inspired picture and posting on your story?*

*Do you truly appreciate the work of Dostoevsky even when most of the time you don't understand whatever he is talking about or do you like the image of being seen as someone who reads classic literature?*

*Do you really enjoy that small R&B artist or do you like to be seen as someone with unique taste?*

Because life is about living, not performing. If we don't enjoy what we do, then what is the point of it at all?

The number of likes, the perfectly curated feed or the reposts do not define us, nor does it place us in any real social hierarchy. What truly matters is what we genuinely enjoy, whether that is singing, gardening, walking, yoga, eating, an obsession with certain web series, the music that everyone loves, or the one internet trolls you for liking.

There is nothing especially "cool" about rejecting trends or following them. I would love to see you be influenced by the internet and discover something you love.

As long as you follow what you love, what gives you satisfaction, what brings you calm when no one is watching, you will be the coolest in any room because there is nothing cooler than simply enjoying your life without needing it to be validated.
